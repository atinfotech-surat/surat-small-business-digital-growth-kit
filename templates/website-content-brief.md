# Website content brief

**Purpose:** Give a business owner, designer, writer, or agency enough context to plan useful website content. Duplicate this file for each project. Skip questions that don't apply.

## 1. Business basics

- Business name customers know: 
- Legal name (only if needed for the project; keep private): 
- Main contact / role: 
- Public phone / WhatsApp / email: 
- Public website or social links: 
- Location customers can visit (if applicable): 
- Service area / delivery area: 
- Opening hours / appointment process: 

## 2. The goal

- The main thing a visitor should do: 
- A second useful action: 
- What counts as a qualified enquiry/order/booking? 
- How will the team respond, and within what realistic timeframe? 
- What is the current obstacle the website should address? 

## 3. Customers

- Who is the main customer? Describe a real situation, not just an age range: 
- What are they trying to solve, buy, or decide? 
- What questions or concerns do they have before contacting you? 
- Which languages should the site support? Who will review translations? 
- Are there accessibility or low-bandwidth needs to plan for? 

## 4. Offer and differentiation

- Main services/products (start with up to five): 
- What is included / not included? 
- Price, price range, or how to request a quote: 
- What makes the offer genuinely different? 
- Which claims can you prove with a source, certificate, or approved example? 
- What should the website never promise? 

## 5. Pages and content

Mark the pages needed:

- [ ] Home
- [ ] Services / products
- [ ] About / team
- [ ] Work / case studies (with written approval)
- [ ] FAQ
- [ ] Contact / booking / enquiry
- [ ] Location / service area
- [ ] Policies (privacy, shipping, returns, cancellations, etc., as applicable)
- [ ] Other: 

For each important page:

- Visitor question this page answers: 
- Key facts to include: 
- Main action / CTA: 
- Images, diagrams, or documents needed (and who owns them): 

## 6. Trust and proof

- Reviews or testimonials approved for use (attach permission/source): 
- Certifications, memberships, or awards (verify date and issuer): 
- Case studies and approved outcome data: 
- Team/owner details and photo permissions: 
- Any confidentiality restrictions: 

Never add a testimonial, client logo, result, or credential just because it sounds persuasive. If proof is unavailable, use a factual explanation of the process instead.

## 7. Brand and practical constraints

- Tone (choose 3–5 words): 
- Words/phrases to use or avoid: 
- Preferred colours / existing brand guide: 
- Existing domain, hosting, CMS, or booking system: 
- Who owns passwords and account recovery? (Do not write passwords here.)
- Budget / timeline / launch date: 
- Person responsible for approving content: 

## 8. After launch

- Who will update hours, prices, photos, or stock? 
- How often can the team review the information? 
- What 1–3 measures will you track for the first month? 
- What is the review date?
