# Product and business photo shot list

Use this for a phone or camera shoot. Aim to show customers what they will actually receive or experience. This is a planning aid, not a substitute for a photographer where one is needed.

## Before the shoot

- [ ] List the products, services, people, or spaces customers most need to understand.
- [ ] Clean the product and the background; remove personal paperwork and unrelated clutter.
- [ ] Use soft daylight if available. Avoid mixed colour temperatures and harsh glare.
- [ ] Photograph the actual item, colour, size, packaging, and finish. Don't retouch a flaw in a way that misleads.
- [ ] Ask permission from staff, customers, partners, or property owners before using identifiable images.
- [ ] Decide where the images will appear: website hero, product cards, maps listing, social posts, or catalogue.

## Shot list

- [ ] **Wide context:** shop, studio, workspace, or product in a realistic setting.
- [ ] **Clear hero:** one primary product/service, uncluttered, with space for a crop.
- [ ] **Detail:** material, finish, ingredients, craft, or distinctive feature.
- [ ] **Scale:** product next to a familiar object or in a real room; keep size truthful.
- [ ] **In use:** show how the product/service is used, with consent where people appear.
- [ ] **Options:** available colours, sizes, variations, or packages—label accurately.
- [ ] **Process:** a real step in making, preparing, or delivering the service.
- [ ] **Packaging / handover:** what the customer actually receives.
- [ ] **Team:** owner or staff at work, if they agree to be shown.
- [ ] **Location:** entrance, sign, or interior, only if customers can visit and you want the location public.
- [ ] **Seasonal update:** current menu, event, or seasonal item, with a date to remove/update it.
- [ ] **Accessibility detail:** entrance, seating, parking, or facilities, described only if verified.

## Simple capture checklist

- [ ] Wipe the camera lens and tap to focus.
- [ ] Take both portrait and landscape versions where they will be useful.
- [ ] Keep important details away from the very edge so the image can be cropped.
- [ ] Check for readable labels, private information, other people's faces, and reflections before sharing.
- [ ] Keep an original copy and a web-ready copy; name files descriptively (for example `handmade-blue-mug-side-view.jpg`).
- [ ] Add a short, factual caption and useful alt text. Don't stuff keywords into alt text.
- [ ] Record photographer/owner and any usage permission needed.

## Alt-text prompt

Describe the essential visible information in one short sentence. Example: “Blue ceramic water bottle beside a steel lunch box on a wooden desk.” Don't start with “image of”; don't claim a feature that isn't visible or verified.
