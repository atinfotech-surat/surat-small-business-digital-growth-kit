# Local search profile guide

A careful, accurate profile helps nearby customers understand your business. This guide focuses on the Google Business Profile; other directories have their own rules. First check Google's current eligibility and representation guidelines in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md).

## Step 1: Confirm the business is eligible and the account is controlled

- [ ] Check whether your business qualifies for a profile under Google's current guidelines. Businesses generally need to make in-person contact with customers during stated hours; don't create a profile just to target a city.
- [ ] Make sure a business owner or trusted staff member has primary access and recovery options.
- [ ] Search for existing profiles before creating another. Duplicate or old listings can confuse customers.
- [ ] Decide whether the public should see a storefront address or a service area. Don't publish a private address unless it is appropriate and you want customers to see it.

## Step 2: Make the core details accurate

- [ ] Use the real-world business name as customers see it on signage and other business material. Don't append search keywords or city names unless they are genuinely part of the name.
- [ ] Choose the smallest number of accurate categories that describe the main business.
- [ ] Add the correct phone, website, hours, holiday hours, and service details.
- [ ] If customers visit, check the address and map pin. If you travel to customers, check Google's current service-area rules.
- [ ] Write a short description that explains the business in plain language. Avoid repeated keywords and unsupported claims.
- [ ] Keep the website and listing consistent; correct outdated information promptly.

## Step 3: Add helpful, genuine visuals and details

- [ ] Use recent photos of the actual storefront, team, products, work, or service process.
- [ ] Get permission from identifiable people and customers before publishing their images.
- [ ] Add only details that help customers decide—such as appointment method, accessibility information, menu/service list, or delivery area—when accurate.
- [ ] Review the profile from a customer’s phone: can they find the address/service area, hours, contact route, and next step?

## Step 4: Request and respond to reviews responsibly

You may ask for an **honest** review from customers with a genuine experience. Don't offer payment, discounts, or gifts; don't ask only customers you expect to be positive; don't pressure people to write a particular rating or words. Use the neutral copy in [`../templates/customer-review-request.md`](../templates/customer-review-request.md). Google's current policy is linked in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md).

When responding:

- Thank the reviewer without revealing private customer information.
- Be specific and calm. Acknowledge the issue and offer an appropriate private follow-up route.
- Don't argue, threaten, or ask someone to remove a review in exchange for a benefit.
- If a review appears to break platform policy, use the platform's reporting process rather than retaliating.

## Step 5: Maintain it monthly

1. Confirm hours, phone, service area, and links are still right.
2. Add a genuine update only when there is useful new information.
3. Check available profile performance data and note calls, website clicks, or direction actions if those are relevant and available.
4. Compare with your own prior period, noting seasonal events and changes in opening hours.
5. Pick one action for next month; don't judge the profile only by a single search position.

## What not to promise

Google describes local results as depending mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence. You cannot pay Google to improve organic local ranking, and a checklist cannot guarantee a position, traffic, or sales. See the official tips in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md).
