# Website launch checklist for a small business

Use this checklist with a new website or when reviewing an existing one. Mark **N/A** where an item genuinely doesn't apply. It is a practical review—not a legal, security, accessibility, or SEO audit.

## 1. Goal and ownership

- [ ] Write one primary goal: enquiry, booking, purchase, visit, or another measurable action.
- [ ] Name the main audience and the area you serve.
- [ ] Decide what a good enquiry looks like and who will respond to it.
- [ ] Confirm the business—not only a former contractor—can access the domain, hosting, website platform, analytics, and listing accounts.
- [ ] Keep account recovery details current and use two-step verification where available.

## 2. Business information and content

- [ ] Business name matches the name customers see on your real-world branding.
- [ ] Phone, WhatsApp, email, address/service area, hours, and holiday hours are accurate.
- [ ] Services/products, prices or price ranges, availability, delivery, returns, and booking terms are current.
- [ ] The homepage quickly explains who you help, what you offer, where you work, and what to do next.
- [ ] Every important page has one clear next action.
- [ ] Use specific, plain-language headings; remove placeholder copy and unsupported superlatives.
- [ ] Testimonials, certifications, client logos, statistics, and case studies are genuine and approved for use.
- [ ] Add a useful FAQ based on questions real customers ask.

## 3. Pages and navigation

- [ ] Home page introduces the business and directs visitors to the main action.
- [ ] Services/products page describes what is included and who it is for.
- [ ] About page has accurate business/team details.
- [ ] Contact page shows working contact routes and realistic response expectations.
- [ ] Location or service-area information is clear without exposing a private address.
- [ ] Privacy, terms, shipping, cancellation, or returns information is present where relevant.
- [ ] Navigation labels are understandable; no links lead to empty or unfinished pages.

## 4. Phone, accessibility, and usability

- [ ] Test on at least one iPhone-sized and one Android-sized screen, not only a desktop.
- [ ] Text is readable without pinching; buttons and links are easy to tap.
- [ ] Every page can be navigated with a keyboard; focus is visible.
- [ ] Images that communicate information have useful text alternatives; decorative images don't repeat nearby copy.
- [ ] Video has captions or a text summary when people need its information.
- [ ] Colour is not the only way information is communicated; text/background contrast is comfortable.
- [ ] Forms have clear labels, explain required fields, and show a helpful confirmation or error.
- [ ] Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to find the phone number, service, price/quote path, and location.

For a deeper review, use the W3C WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md).

## 5. Photos, speed, and trust

- [ ] Photos show the actual business, products, team, or process—not unrelated stock imagery presented as real.
- [ ] Get consent before publishing identifiable people, customer spaces, or testimonials.
- [ ] Crop images thoughtfully and compress large files while keeping important detail clear.
- [ ] Check that images load on mobile data and that the page doesn't jump around while loading.
- [ ] Use HTTPS; remove test logins, demo contact details, placeholder domains, and development banners.
- [ ] If the site collects customer details, collect only what you need and explain how enquiries are handled.

## 6. Search and local discovery basics

- [ ] Each important page has a clear, unique title and useful description.
- [ ] The page heading describes the page; headings are not chosen just to repeat search phrases.
- [ ] Address, phone, opening hours, and business name agree across your website and business listings.
- [ ] Service-area statements are truthful and specific enough to help customers decide.
- [ ] Important content is written as text, not only baked into an image.
- [ ] Search Console or other analytics are installed only if you have access, understand the data, and have handled any required privacy notices/consent.
- [ ] Confirm the intended indexing setting: a demo/staging site may need to stay out of search; a public business site should not accidentally be set to `noindex`.

Search visibility is not guaranteed. Avoid keyword stuffing or anyone promising a particular position. See the official Google Search and local-profile references in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md).

## 7. Contact and conversion test

- [ ] Tap every phone, email, map, WhatsApp, booking, and social link on a phone.
- [ ] Submit every form once with a test enquiry; confirm the right person receives it.
- [ ] Check the thank-you message and follow-up route.
- [ ] Use a test purchase/booking only if the platform supports a safe test mode.
- [ ] Make sure the primary action is visible without a visitor having to hunt for it.

## 8. Final launch and first month

- [ ] Check spelling, dates, prices, claims, and links one more time.
- [ ] Save a backup or confirm you can restore the site.
- [ ] Confirm the production domain points to the intended website before announcing it.
- [ ] Remove temporary `noindex` only when the production site is ready to be discovered.
- [ ] Record a baseline for enquiries, calls/bookings, and the site measure you actually care about.
- [ ] Review the first month in [`03-monthly-digital-checkup.md`](03-monthly-digital-checkup.md); choose one change at a time.
