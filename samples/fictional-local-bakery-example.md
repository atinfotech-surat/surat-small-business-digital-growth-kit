# Worked example: fictional neighbourhood bakery

**This is an invented example, not a real Surat business or client.** It shows how to fill in the brief and choose a few actions. Replace it with your own facts.

## Business snapshot

- Business name: Example Bakery (fictional)
- Audience: nearby households looking for celebration cakes and weekday snacks
- Main service: made-to-order cakes; walk-in items vary by day
- Area: a fictional neighbourhood in Surat; exact address intentionally omitted
- Primary action: call or message to ask about availability and pickup timing
- Constraint: the team has one phone and limited time to create posts

## Website message

> Celebration cakes and fresh bakery favourites, made to order in [neighbourhood]. Ask us about flavours, size, availability, and pickup before you visit.

This message is useful because it tells a customer what is offered and what they should confirm. It does not invent a delivery promise, ingredient claim, price, or opening hour.

## Possible website sections

1. Home: what the bakery makes and the best next step.
2. Cakes: current options, size guide, order lead time, and how to enquire.
3. Everyday favourites: items that are actually available; date the menu if it changes.
4. About: real team/story with consent.
5. Contact: current hours, verified location, phone/message route.
6. FAQ: how far ahead to order, pickup window, customisation, payment method, and allergen questions answered by the bakery.

## First month: choose only three posts

- **Week 1:** A clear photo of a real cake, plus how to ask about size and pickup.
- **Week 2:** A short behind-the-scenes clip of a real decorating step; get staff consent.
- **Week 3:** Answer one frequent question, such as how much notice is needed. Confirm the answer with the team first.

If there is time, add a fourth post about the shop's current hours or location. Don't post a menu, price, or “available today” claim unless it is checked that day.

## What to measure

- Number of cake availability enquiries.
- Number of orders confirmed.
- How customers found the bakery, asked as an optional question.
- Common questions or points of confusion.

A single month is not enough to prove cause and effect. Record what changed and use the notes to improve the next month.
